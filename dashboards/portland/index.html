Building Condition

Find a high school. See its condition.

Every high school is scored on how much repair work it needs relative to what it would cost to replace the whole building. Schools already modernized score near zero. Schools waiting their turn score high — and those are next in line for bond dollars.

Facility Condition Index (FCI) — the ratio of a building's estimated repair cost to its current replacement value — plotted per high school site, per Volume 1 of the district's 2021 Long-Range Facility Plan.

Cluster Need

Where repair costs are catching up to replacement value

Each high school cluster's buildings are compared two ways: what it would cost to fix everything that's broken today, versus what it would cost to just rebuild from scratch. When those two numbers get close, replacement usually becomes the smarter long-term investment.

Cluster-level replacement value vs. documented repair cost, per Volume 1 Figure 9 (Facility Condition by Cluster), 2021 Long-Range Facility Plan.

Where the Bond Dollars Go

Following the 2020 bond — and who's still waiting

Voters approved $1.2 billion in 2020. Here's what it's actually paying for — most of it goes toward rebuilding and modernizing schools, not new programs or administration. And here's how far that's already gotten: six of nine high schools are already modernized or brand new.

Enrollment vs. Space

Some schools are packed. Others have room to spare.

Every school building has a fixed number of seats. This shows how many students are actually enrolled against that seat count — the gray track is the building's capacity, the colored bar is real students. When the bar runs past the end of the track, the school is over capacity.

Functional capacity (seats) per Volume 1/2 site data, plotted against enrollment derived from projected utilization %, 2021-22 through 2025-26 (range shown = 5-year min–max). Source: Volume 1 Enrollment & Utilization tables.
