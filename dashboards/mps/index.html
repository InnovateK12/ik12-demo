Tier 1 · Early Intervention for Students
Escalation path before a student ever reaches Tier 2
Step 1 — Positive Attendance Phone CallElementary: 2–4 absences/month · MS/HS: 3–5 absences/month. Documented as PLP-Positive Attendance Phone Call.
Step 2 — 2x10 Intervention2 minutes/day for 10 consecutive school days with the classroom teacher. Positive call home on day 1 and day 10.
Tier 2 — Building Intervention Team referralScreened for Check-In/Check-Out; may progress to a Social Academic Instructional Group.

03 · Culture, Climate & Alternatives to Suspension

Five components of school climate, one continuum of support

Community building comes first — then proactive practices, response to behavior, Building Intervention Team support, and administrator alternatives to suspension.

1

Relationships & Community

Strong sense of welcoming; minds, hearts, bodies, and spirit supported and valued.

2

Expectations & Procedures

Predictable routines and shared decision-making between staff, students, and families.

3

Skill Development

Cultivating student strengths, wellness, self-regulation, and reflection.

4

Responding to Behavior

Redirection and accountability held alongside repair — never one without the other.

5

Systems of Acknowledgement

Celebrating individual and group success; promoting joy throughout the building.

Restorative Practices Continuum
The toolkit's 7 continuum steps, shown as equal segments for illustration — not measured implementation shares
Behavior T-Chart — classroom-managed vs. office-managed
Every school maintains this chart so staff apply consistent standards

Classroom-managed

  • Leaving classroom without permission
  • Arguing / disrespect / profanity
  • Refusing work or orders
  • Off-task behavior, sleeping, running
  • Name-calling / teasing, electronics use

Office-managed

  • Chronic disrespect / disruption
  • Weapons, battery, fighting
  • Bullying, harassment, or assault
  • Gang-related activity
  • Vandalism, false alarms
04 · Ambitious Instruction

Three levers, accelerating learning

Explicit Instruction and Formative Practices meet at Engagement — hands-on, minds-on learning for every student, every day.

Explicit InstructionIntentional, purposeful teaching that centers student meaning and sense-making, connected to prior learning.
Formative PracticesA process to elicit and use evidence of student thinking, building self-directed learners.
Engagement — hands-on, minds-on

Academic Standard of Care
10 non-negotiables guiding every classroom
1Build positive relationships with students & families
2Know what each student needs, in each content area
3Conduct formative assessments regularly
4Differentiate instruction for students
5Instruct students at or above grade level
6Utilize scaffolding above & below grade level
7Design instruction from the content standards
8Use district adopted/endorsed materials
9Center learning around student voice & choice
10Design inquiry-based learning experiences
05 · Equity & Disproportionality

Interrupting bias at every vulnerable decision point

Six best practices — from defining behaviors consistently to re-entry after discipline — anchor how MPS addresses discipline disproportionality.

Define & categorize behaviors

Shared, explicit definitions across staff, students, and families.

Talk about race

Courageous Conversations protocols create space for brave dialogue.

Engage student voice

Students help create expectations, bulletin boards, and announcements.

Interrupt vulnerable decision points

Stereotype replacement, perspective-taking, mindfulness, stepping away.

Re-entry after discipline

Every student is welcomed back and reconnected to missed learning.

Anti-racist universal supports

Active, conscious efforts to challenge institutional racial power.
